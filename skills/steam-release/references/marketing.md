# Wishlists, demo, launch

Everything here is an estimate from the indie scene, not a Valve number. Mark it that way in answers.
Main sources: Chris Zukowski (https://howtomarketagame.com, benchmarks: https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/) and GameDiscoverCo (https://newsletter.gamediscover.co). Numbers shift, so re-read the benchmarks page before setting a target.

## Wishlist targets (Zukowski, as of June 2026)

| Tier | Wishlists at launch |
|---|---|
| Bronze | 5,000 |
| Silver | 8,000 |
| Gold | 50,000 |
| Diamond | 90,000 |

- Around 7,000 wishlists is the usual estimate for entering "Popular Upcoming". Since June 2026 the tab covers roughly a month instead of a week, so the bar is higher.
- Conversion: in the first week roughly 10 to 20 % of wishlisters buy. The spread between games is huge.
- Rough revenue estimate: wishlists × 0.15 × price × 0.7 (Valve's 30 % share), before tax and refunds. Give it as an order of magnitude only.

## Collecting wishlists

Ordered by effort and impact for solo developers:

1. **Store page up early.** Every mention needs a place to wishlist. Without a page, attention is wasted.
2. **Steam festivals:** Next Fest once per game. On top, look for themed Steam fests (for example simulation or management) in the Steamworks event calendar and register. Free and targeted.
3. **Short videos** (TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Reddit): one clip shows one satisfying activity in 10 to 20 seconds. For sims, "bringing order" works well (stacking boxes, a filling warehouse, a truck leaving on time).
4. **Reddit:** fitting subreddits (for example r/simulationgames, r/IndieGaming, or the engine's subreddit) with a GIF instead of ad copy. Read each sub's self-promotion rules.
5. **Streamers and YouTubers:** small and mid-sized channels that play similar games. A personal email with a key and 3 sentences about the game. Track them in a spreadsheet.
6. **Press:** a short email with a press kit. For indies, press usually brings less than streamers.
7. **Devlog and newsletter:** use Steam announcements on the page, they reach followers.

What usually brings little: paid ads before launch, a Discord server without a community, posts without visuals.

## Demo and Next Fest

- A demo almost always pays off for simulations, because the feel only clicks when playing.
- Content: the first hour with a clear closing scene and a "Wishlist" button in the demo's main menu. Not the whole game.
- Timing: release the demo 1 to 3 months before Next Fest to collect bugs and feedback first.
- Next Fest works best 1 to 4 months before launch. Too early and the boost fades, too late and there is no buffer for feedback.
- During the fest: stream daily (Steam broadcast on the page), reply to every piece of feedback, ship bug fixes fast.

## Launch

- **Date:** not during big Steam sales and not in weeks with big releases in the same genre. Many indies launch Monday to Thursday, timed for the US morning.
- **Price:** look at 5 to 10 comparable games of similar scope. Better to start slightly higher with a 10 to 20 % launch discount than too low. Take Valve's regional pricing suggestions.
- **Early Access** only if the game is already fun and regular updates are realistic. Switching to full release gives a second launch moment.
- **First week:** fix bugs right away, reply to reviews. A small update based on feedback shows the game is alive.
- **Reviews:** the score only appears from 10 reviews. Keys for streamers and testers before launch help reach that quickly. Never buy reviews or trade them for rewards, Valve forbids it.

## After launch

- 30 days without discounts (except the launch discount). Then seasonal sales and themed fests.
- Bigger updates with a Steam announcement and trailer bring visibility back.
- Don't waste wishlist emails: at most one every 2 weeks, discount at least 20 %.
